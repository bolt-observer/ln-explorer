bolt.observer: revamp directions

These are low-fidelity wireframes. They show layout, hierarchy and interaction model, not final visuals. Grey bars stand for text, and the accent colour marks emphasis or selection. Red pins match the notes on the right. The full audit is in AUDIT.md.

Chosen direction: A · Atlas. High-fidelity mockups are in atlas/index.html.

A · Atlas

The graph is the app. It runs edge to edge, the controls float over it, and everything else opens as an inspector or sheet on top of the map, so you never lose your place.

Best if
The graph is the brand and the reason people visit. You want a memorable, "instrument panel" feel.
Strength
Clicking a node shows its neighbourhood in place. Exploration feels continuous, and it looks clearly different from mempool or amboss.
Tradeoff
It's the hardest to make accessible and to use on mobile. Table-heavy work (filtering 5k nodes) feels secondary. WebGL performance becomes a UX dependency.

B · Ledger

Quiet and typographic, like a well-made reference site. You start with a search box and a handful of headline numbers. Node pages read like documents, and the graph becomes one destination among several.

Best if
Most visitors arrive with a pubkey or alias to look up, and you want the most trustworthy, readable result.
Strength
Fastest route to an answer. Best accessibility and mobile behaviour. Prose summaries ("#3 by capacity") help newcomers. Light theme stands out in a dark-mode-heavy niche.
Tradeoff
Less spectacle. The graph loses its hero spot. Power users filtering thousands of nodes may want more density than this gives by default.

Tradeoff
It looks like many SaaS dashboards, so it's less distinctive. Mobile needs a separate layout. It can be intimidating for casual visitors.

Shared across every direction

  1. Global typeahead search (alias, pubkey, SCID, NxNxN). An exact match opens directly.
  2. One unit rule: BTC with 2 decimals in the UI, exact sats on hover and copy. Tabular numerals everywhere.
  3. Colour has meaning: one accent for selection and interaction, a positive/negative pair for deltas. Links are ink.
  4. Methodology and source caveats move to ⓘ popovers and a /methodology page.
  5. Empty states collapse to one line. Columns with no data hide.
  6. Flat surfaces: hairlines or tonal steps, no drop shadows, no nested cards.
  7. Skeleton loaders sized like the final content, so nothing jumps.
  8. Graph labels only on hubs and on hover. Add a legend. Selection highlights the neighbourhood.
  9. Channel page drawn by direction: A ⇄ B, each policy next to its arrow.
  10. Mobile: one-row header, sheet or tab navigation, card rows instead of wide tables.
